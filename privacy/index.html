Privacy

Privacy policy

Last updated 26 October 2026

This page explains what Sova stores, why, who can see it and what you can ask us to do with it. It describes Sova as it works today. Sova is open as a demo for now: sign-in works through “Try the demo”, Google, an email code, and a short list of test phone numbers. We will update this page before opening to everyone.

The short version

  • Sova never holds, moves, lends or invests your money. Members pay each other directly; Sova keeps the record.
  • We store what a savings record needs: your phone number or email, name, bank details, a hashed PIN, and receipt photos you choose to add.
  • Members of your circles see your name and phone number (or email, if you signed in with email or Google), and your bank details when it is your turn to collect. The public circle record shows only an id and your first name and initial.
  • No ads, no analytics, no trackers. We don't sell your data.
  • You can ask to see, correct or delete your data under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023.

01Who we are

Sova is a record-keeping service for ajo, esusu and adashe savings circles, built in Nigeria. “Sova”, “we” and “us” mean the people who run it. For anything on this page, write to adeyanjufuhad@gmail.com.

Sova is not a bank, lender or wallet. It never holds, moves, lends or invests money and does not process payments. Members pay each other by bank transfer or cash, and Sova records what happened.

02What we collect

Phone number
If you sign in with your phone: to sign you in with a text code, and so members of your circles can reach you. Stored as +234XXXXXXXXXX.
Email address
If you sign in with an email code or with Google: to sign you in, and members of your circles see it where they would see a phone number. Stored in lower case.
Google account
If you choose “Continue with Google”, Google confirms it is you and tells Sova your email address, your name (to fill in your profile, which you can change) and an account id. Sova never sees your Google password and gets no other access to your Google account.
Name
Shown to members of your circles. The public circle record shows only your first name and initial.
Bank details
Bank name, 10-digit account number and account name, used only to show members where to pay you when it is your turn to collect. Sova never debits, credits or checks your account. Changing them needs your PIN.
PIN
Stored only as an argon2id hash, so nobody at Sova can read it. It confirms money actions such as marking a payment or confirming a payout.
Circle activity
Circles you start or join, rules you accept, who vouched for you, payments you mark as sent or confirm, payouts, disputes, votes, comments, swaps and handovers.
Receipt photos
Only if you attach one to a payment. Kept in a private storage bucket; members of that circle view it through links that expire after 5 minutes.
Ask Sova questions
If you use Ask Sova, your question and the facts of the circle or dispute it is about (amounts, rules, turn order and payment states, with people as first name and initial) are sent to the AI model that writes the answer. Phone numbers, bank details, PINs and receipt photos are never sent. Questions and answers are not stored; only a count of answers per person per day is kept, for the daily limit.
Sova Score
Worked out from your circle record. If you choose to share it, a snapshot is published behind a random link (see “Who can see what”).
Sign-in records
Sign-in codes sent to your phone or email are stored only as a keyed hash and expire after 5 minutes. Sessions are stored as a hash of the token, with the description your browser or app sends (its user agent), and last up to 30 days unless you sign out.
Notifications
In-app messages about your circles. Sova does not send marketing texts or emails.
Waitlist
If you join it on the website: your name, phone number, whether you are a member, admin or collector, and, if you give them, your city and circle size.
Server logs
When your device talks to the Sova API, the request (address, time, IP address) is logged for security and fixing problems. PINs, codes and tokens are removed from logs.

03How we use it

  • To run your circles: turns, payments, payouts, rules, disputes, swaps and reminders.
  • To show each member what they need, such as where to pay and who has paid.
  • To keep accounts safe: limits on sign-in codes and PIN attempts, and spotting a stolen sign-in.
  • To work out your Sova Score.
  • To tell people on the waitlist when Sova opens.

We don't use your data for advertising, we don't sell or rent it, and we don't give it to lenders.

Under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, we rely on: providing the service you asked for (your account and circles); your consent for optional things (receipt photos, sharing your score, the waitlist), which you can withdraw; and our legitimate interest in keeping Sova secure (logs and rate limits).

04Who can see what

  • Members of your circles see your full name and phone number (or your email, if you signed in with email or Google), your payments and their status, receipt photos you attach in that circle, and disputes and votes. When you are the collector for the current turn, they also see your bank details so they know where to pay.
  • Anyone with a circle's link can download and check that circle's tamper-evident record on the verify page. People appear in it only as an opaque id and first name plus initial (for example “Ada O.”). Phone numbers, emails and bank details are never written to it. A circle's link works like a private link, so share it with care.
  • Anyone at all can read the public ledger anchors: every 6 hours, each circle's latest chain head (a sequence number and a hash) is published to a public GitHub branch, filed under a SHA-256 hash of the circle's id. The file contains no names or numbers and can't be used to find circles.
  • Anyone with your score link sees the snapshot you shared: first name and initial, score, the three rates, payment and circle counts, and when you joined. There is one live link at a time, and you can stop sharing in the app whenever you like; the link then says it is no longer active.
  • Everyone using the demo: “Try the demo” gives each visit its own private copy with pretend people, which is deleted after 24 hours. Don't enter real personal or bank information there.
  • Service providers. RumptyCloud hosts the database, API, file storage, web app and website, and runs the open AI model behind Ask Sova on its own servers in Lagos (RumptyCloud says prompts are not used for training). GitHub stores Sova's code and the public anchors, and builds the app and website. Google checks who you are when you continue with Google. Brevo sends email sign-in codes, so it receives your email address and the code. Phone sign-in codes currently work only for a fixed list of test numbers, so no text messages are sent. Before opening to everyone we will use a paid SMS provider, which will receive your phone number and code; we will name it here first.
  • We only share data beyond this if Nigerian law requires it.

05Website, apps and cookies

This website has no ads, analytics or tracking scripts and sets no cookies. Its fonts are served from the site itself. Two things talk to other servers:

  • The waitlist form sends what you type to the Sova API.
  • The verify page downloads the public anchors file from GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com), so GitHub sees your IP address when you use it.

The web app keeps you signed in using the browser tab's session storage, which is cleared when you close the tab; the Android app uses the phone's secure storage. Neither has ads or analytics. Like other Flutter web apps by default, the web app loads its rendering engine from Google's servers (gstatic.com), so Google sees your IP address and browser details when it loads. The sign-in page also loads Google's sign-in button from accounts.google.com, so Google sees the same details there, whether or not you use it.

06How long we keep it

  • Account details: while you have an account, or until you delete it or ask us to.
  • Sign-in codes: 5 minutes. Sessions: up to 30 days, or until you sign out.
  • Circle records: the ledger is append-only by design, so members can rely on it. Its entries can't be edited or deleted, by us or anyone else, and they name people only by id and first name plus initial.
  • Receipt photos: kept with the circle's record, and deleted when you delete your account.
  • Shared scores: when you stop sharing, the snapshot is marked stopped and no longer shown. You can ask us to delete it.
  • Waitlist: until we contact you about launch, or until you ask to be removed.
  • Server logs: kept by our hosting provider and used only for security and fixing problems.
  • Demo data is fictional and is reset from time to time.

07Your rights

Under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 you can ask us to:

  • tell you what data we hold about you and give you a copy;
  • correct data that is wrong;
  • delete your data, or stop or limit how we use it;
  • give you your data in a format you can take elsewhere;
  • stop using data you gave with consent (for example, a shared score or the waitlist).

You can change your name and bank details yourself in the app, and delete your account from the Record tab (Delete account, with your PIN) once none of your circles is running. Circles that haven't started are left for you; if you started one, cancel it first. Deleting it removes your name, phone number, email, Google account id, PIN, bank details, sessions, notifications, score history and receipt photos; payments in finished circles stay on those circles' records without your name or number, and ledger entries stay as described above. The phone number or email you sign in with can't be changed in the app. For anything else, write to adeyanjufuhad@gmail.com from or about the number on your account, and we will check it is yours before acting.

If you are unhappy with how we handle your data, you can complain to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission.

08Security

  • PINs are hashed with argon2id. Five wrong PINs lock the account for 15 minutes.
  • Sign-in codes and PIN checks are rate-limited; codes are stored only as a keyed hash.
  • Sign-in tokens are stored only as hashes and change on every refresh; reusing an old one signs that device out.
  • Receipt photos sit in a private bucket and open only through links that expire after 5 minutes.
  • The apps and website talk to the API over HTTPS.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your data, we will tell you and the Nigeria Data Protection Commission as the Act requires. Sova will never call or text to ask for your PIN or sign-in code.

09Children

Sova is for people aged 18 and over. We don't knowingly hold data about children.
